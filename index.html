Sixty-two hours of interviews come before day one

A senior engineer costs $18,290 a month fully loaded, $219,480 a year, $105.52 for every hour on the clock. Before a new hire writes a line of code, the company pays for the funnel that found them. One hiring round: 150 resumes screened at six minutes each is 15 hours, 30 phone screens at 45 minutes including notes is 22.5 hours, 10 take-home reviews at 40 minutes is 6.7 hours, five onsite loops staffed with three interviewers cost 15 interview hours, and five half-hour debriefs close it out at 2.5 more. That is 61.7 hours of loaded senior time, $6,507 per hire, 7.7 working days of the team's year spent selecting a person.

Rarely one hire. Four hires a year, a modest pace for a growing team, runs the ledger to 247 hours and $26,028, 43 percent of the $59,940 yearly plan spent finding people before the first of them ships anything. The funnel also gates the calendar: a req opens, screens trickle in over weeks, and the roadmap waits on the pipeline while the seat sits empty.

The subscription prices the work directly. Poket Dev is $5,995 a month with unlimited requests, one active card on a Trello board at a time: onboarding lands in about a day, each card ships inside 72 hours, revisions run until the card passes, and cancellation leaves the private repository with you. The interview ledger for four hires buys 4.3 months of the plan, and the card that starts this week is the one the funnel would still be screening for.

The workflow and pricing sit at Poket Dev, an unlimited software development subscription that ships in cards instead of hiring rounds.
